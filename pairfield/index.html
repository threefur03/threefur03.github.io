PAIRFIELD

Precedence-Aware Conflict Potentials
for Mapless Navigation among Heterogeneous Road Users

Anonymous Authors

A sparse precedence graph estimates who passes each conflict first. A conflict potential with temporal commitment then sets the ego's speed behind an unchanged safety filter, reducing the hard braking imposed on pedestrians, cyclists and drivers.

Core contributions

PAIRFIELD treats precedence, i.e. which road user passes a conflict point first, as an explicit and calibrated variable on a sparse conflict graph. The graph is learned from 2,432 recorded crossing conflicts in German and Chinese drone datasets. In those recordings, vehicles that pass first make vulnerable road users brake hard in 14.0% of conflicts; in the reverse direction the figure is 1.0%.

The planner selects a speed along a coarse reference path, without an HD map. It minimises a conflict potential made of three terms: precedence uncertainty, the discomfort its choice imposes on others, and its own braking. Total-variation and decrease terms make it commit to a precedence decision instead of hesitating. An unchanged barrier filter keeps safety separate from the social model. In closed-loop CARLA, the method is compared under one contract with ten cited planners, including CARLA's own agents. Evaluation covers ground-truth perception with occlusion, onboard camera and LiDAR, and safety-critical events. The same code runs on a LIMO robot.

Method

Framework

Interaction risk depends on who approaches, in which direction and along which future path. PAIRFIELD represents these distinctions through ordered receiver/source classes and body-aligned anisotropy. Its gradient supplies pairwise social cues and directed edge salience; future positions supply an anticipatory cue. Coordinate-frame choices and forecast backends are declared with each experimental configuration.

Road execution projects interaction cues onto the assigned route and regulates target speed within that route. A CBF-QP filters the shaped reference before tracking.

Controlled evaluation

Planar mechanisms and constrained-space navigation

Planar simulation separates passing geometry, anticipation and wait/recommit control under known agent states.

One method family, two forecast configurations: PAIRFIELD (linear forecast) uses constant-velocity extrapolation; PAIRFIELD (graph forecast) uses the learned interaction graph. The primary planar suite uses linear forecasts. CARLA's source-body experiments use the learned graph predictor.

Performance varies by layout. The tests below separate class-conditioned passing geometry from the wait/recommit controller's constrained-passage behavior; complete baseline and ablation results remain expandable.

Closed-loop CARLA

Compared methods

Ten published planners and three ablations run under one contract in CARLA. Each method controls speed along a reference path, without an HD map. All methods receive the same perceived road users and use the same plant model. All pass through the same safety filter (stopping-distance barrier and AEB), so each comparison isolates the nominal decision of who goes first. A dagger (†) marks an adapted re-implementation: the cited method is rebuilt from its publication or source code and reformulated for a speed-only, path-following ego.

Mapless CARLA evaluation

Hard braking imposed on surrounding road users

The ego receives a reference path and its own perception; no HD map or prior static map is provided. Road users include pedestrians, cyclists, cars, vans, trucks and buses, and each responds to the ego through a yield-or-go decision with class-specific deceleration. The primary endpoint is the number of road users the ego forces to brake hard, at least twice their class reference deceleration and before the ego has passed.

Each replay re-renders a logged episode from four views: a top-down render with the interaction graph and the method's field on a blue-to-red scale, the front camera, a chase camera and the roof LiDAR. Edges connect the ego to road users whose paths cross its corridor ahead; red edges mark road users the ego is expected to pass first. Rings mark road users forced to brake hard. APF is shown with its Gaussian potential field and PAIRFIELD with its conflict potential along the route; CBF-QP and BehaviorAgent† have no field.

Safety-critical events in closed loop

Occlusion, cut-ins, cyclists, emergency stops and reversals

Six families of hazards adapted from SOTIF logical test cases start at a set time-to-arrival of the ego. Each hazard reacts once it sees the ego: it may brake, steer away, stop, step back or turn back. Vans, trucks and buses hide road users from the ego's line of sight.

104 scenes, all 14 methods, ground truth with occlusion. PAIRFIELD forces 0.96 hard brakes per episode, against 1.04–1.50 for the baselines, with no collisions. The difference is significant against nine baselines and not against PDM-Lite† (Holm p = 0.88). Mapless ROS 2 / LIMO navigation

Following controlled planar tests and closed-loop CARLA evaluation, the LIMO deployment connects onboard sensing, field evaluation and physical motion without a prior map.

From tracked observations to local control

The ROS 2 bridge uses odometry, LaserScan geometry and tracked agents in the local frame. LIMO queries a receiver-aligned class-pair parameter export with optional linear forecasts. A speed governor and soft dynamic-agent / hard static-obstacle QP produce bounded Twist commands through the observed differential-drive interface.
